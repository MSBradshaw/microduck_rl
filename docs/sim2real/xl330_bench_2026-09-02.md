# XL330 bench session — 2026-09-02

What a real single-joint XL330 bench said about the actuator model the whole
sprung-foot campaign has been simulated on. Four sim errors found, all four
corrected in `d387b12b`. One gearbox destroyed getting here.

**Data**: `docs/sim2real/bench_data/xl330_bench_2026-09-02.tar.gz` (53 logs).
Uncompressed working copy at `~/Project/Repo/Pollen/MICRODUCK/bench_data_backup/`.

## Rig

XL330-M288 (model 1200, fw 53), 160 mm printed arm (31 g, CoM ~80 mm), M12
washers at the tip (6.85 g each, weighed). Battery 8.4 V — **above the
documented 6.0 V maximum**, so `hardware_error_status` bit 0 (input voltage)
is permanently set. It is not in the shutdown mask, so it flags without
disabling; do not reboot for it (see "mistakes" below).

Recorded with `scripts/bam_record.py` at ~197 Hz, 0.02 ms jitter. Position
quantum 0.0879° = 1 encoder count; velocity quantum 0.0240 rad/s = 1 tick.

## The four findings

### 1. Gearbox friction supports 75% of external load

Holding **125.0 mN·m** of gravity at 81.5° drew **86 mA**. With the documented
`kt` = 0.366 N·m/A the motor supplied only **31.5 mN·m**, so friction supported
**93.5 mN·m = 75% of the load**. Published `load_friction_external_stribeck`
is 0.081; the measurement implies ~0.75.

Corroborated three ways: a 3-point holding sweep fits `τ = 1.19 mN·m/unit · I +
11 mN·m`; the arm rests anywhere within ±9° of vertical; and at 0 g it does not
backdrive at all.

### 2. Static friction is ~2.5× the model, on two independent specimens

Released at −82.8°, the arm travelled **0.18°** in 4 s against 24.1 mN·m of
gravity torque. A second, undamaged servo: **2.20°**. So static friction
**≥ 24 mN·m** vs `friction_base + friction_stribeck` = 9.45 mN·m published.

Consequence: `lift_and_drop` and `nothing` carry no information at 0 g on any
XL330 — the trajectory's premise is gravity backdriving the joint, and 31 g at
160 mm cannot. They come alive at ≥14 g (46 mN·m).

### 3. The P-gain lever is exhausted by kp ≈ 400

A power-flow analysis predicted **3.1×** more hop from `kp_fw` 200 → 2000, and
this bench existed to validate it. At a fixed 65 g:

| kp | `up_and_down` reached | `sin_time_square` amplitude | peak current |
|---|---|---|---|
| 200 | 70.7° | ±51.6° | 0.39 A |
| 400 | 75.9° | **±65.7°** | 0.67 A |
| 800 | 81.1° | ±65.0° | 0.69 A |
| 1600 | 83.1° | **±59.4°** ↓ | 1.18 A |

An **8× gain increase buys 5% more holding torque**. Dynamic amplitude peaks at
400–800 and **falls** at 1600 while current rises 71%. The prediction is wrong,
and finding 1 says why: the extra torque goes into the geartrain.

### 4. Current Limit does nothing in Position Control Mode

Officially documented (e-manual): Current Limit applies in Current Control and
Current-based Position Control modes only. Confirmed empirically — at
`current_limit` 30, 60 and 120 the arm reached the same 81.5° reporting 85–87
raw units, i.e. **above the limit in every case**.

So the 1.75 A ceiling we treated as gearbox protection has never applied to any
run. Present Current is **1 mA/unit** (address 126); `kt` from the datasheet is
0.345–0.378 across 3.7–6.0 V, so the published 0.366 is right.

## Also established

**Fitting current matters, and position-only fitting hides the error.**
`bam/fit.py` scores position only. On `up_and_down` the published model has
**5.5 mrad** of position error — a seemingly excellent fit — while its current
is **5.7× wrong**. The P loop compensates its way to the right position with
completely wrong torque. `scripts/bam_fit_current.py` adds the channel;
simulated current is recovered as `compute_torque(...) / kt`.

**The chirp excitation is hardware-damaging.** 2.00 A peak = 140% of rated
stall torque, above 1.2 A for 12% of a run, concentrated in its 3.4–5.0 Hz
third. Torque demand grows as `f²` at fixed amplitude, so a constant-amplitude
sweep is inherently damaging at its top end. Nine such runs preceded the
gearbox failure; the 272 g static payload was probably the last straw, not the
cause. `UNSAFE_TRAJECTORIES` in the recorder now gates it.

**A tapered replacement is not worth it** — a Fisher analysis put an
amplitude-tapered sweep at 3.4% against the constant-amplitude version's 81.6%.
The chirp's information *was* the overload.

## Mistakes worth not repeating

- **Rebooting to clear the input-voltage error.** `reboot()` re-initialises the
  position counter; combined with a non-zero `homing_offset` it left
  `present_position` reading 265° (raw 5064, outside 0–4095). The recorder then
  enabled torque and drove toward a 265° error, displacing the arm into the
  frame. Fix: never reboot for bit 0, and validate raw ∈ 0–4095 *before*
  energising.
- **Capping payload on gravity torque instead of motor torque.** On a rising
  move the motor supplies gravity *plus* friction. And the cap must be against
  the gearbox's *rated* stall (0.52 N·m at 5 V), not the torque the motor can
  make at 8.4 V, nor the "usable" figure after friction.
- **Giving an experiment-design optimiser a capability limit and no damage
  limit.** It optimised toward the capability. Its own recommended grid
  (252 g at 200 mm = 0.534 N·m) would have broken a gearbox too.
- **Inferring a register's unit from datasheet arithmetic** instead of reading
  the e-manual. Produced two mutually exclusive answers over several rounds.

## Open

- The BAM refit is **underdetermined** — 6 logs, one payload, one gain. Not run
  to convergence; the parameter values in any interim fit are not trustworthy,
  particularly `load_friction_motor` (0.267 → 0.047), which has been the
  deciding input to three separate decisions.
- **No data at kp 200 or 2000** in the identification set (only 400/800), and
  none below 8.4 V. A voltage sweep with a benchtop supply is the one axis that
  identifies `R`, the worst-determined parameter — and 5.0 V would put finding 1
  on a documented operating point rather than a 40%-over-spec extrapolation.
- Test A (bidirectional staircase, `--trajectory staircase`) was run once at
  65 g. It is the only excitation that measures load-dependent friction
  directly rather than through a payload proxy.
